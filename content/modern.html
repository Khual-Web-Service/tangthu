Modern Zo, Zomi and Chin History: Panglong to Today

Independence placed the Zo in new nation-states. [[iseas-panglong]] The decades since have brought reunification campaigns and, recently, mass displacement. [[wiki-zra]] [[pti2021]] [[asianews]]

{{short}}
  • In 1947, Chin leaders signed the Panglong Agreement that created Burma. [[iseas-panglong]] [[bni-cnd]]
  • On 20 February 1948 they voted to replace chiefs with democracy. That day is Chin National Day. [[chankhar]]
  • Groups like ZORO (1988) work to bring Zo people across borders together peacefully. [[wiki-zoro]]
  • Since Myanmar’s 2021 coup, tens of thousands have fled to Mizoram; violence in Manipur since 2023 has displaced many more. [[asianews]] [[deccan2023]]
{{/short}}

The Second World War {{oral}}

Thang Za Tuan of Mualpi records that in 1942 Pum Za Mang refused to supply labour for a planned Imphal–Tedim motor road unless it ran through Tonzang; the route via Cikha, Tuikhiang, Mualkawi and Nakzang was adopted and finished within a year. His text links the road to “Operation of U-Go” and a “Lt. Col. Mutachi”, as written. [[thangzatuan-pzm]] In 1943 a three-day assembly at Tedim organised by the British officer Kelly founded the Chin Levy, with front-line (A) and home-guard (B) levies and a transport levy. [[thangzatuan-pzm]]

Under Japanese occupation, a Chin Defence Army (CDA) formed under Bo Lian Cin Thang. Its training officer and interpreter, Bo Mang Za Thang (Sian Khaw Zam, 1920–1981), is remembered for persuading the officers Sakamaki and Inada to release the Kahngen chief Pa Dong Za Lian, who had been sentenced to death over a fire at a Japanese post. [[cda-mzt]]

What did the Chin agree at Panglong? {{doc}}

The Agreement of 12 February 1947 was signed by the Chin, Kachin and Shan with Aung San. Chin delegates came from Falam, Tiddim and Haka, and the Chin National Day is celebrated each 20 February. [[iseas-panglong]] [[bni-cnd]] Dates are on the timeline. Burma became independent on 4 January 1948. [[bni-cnd]] [[frontier-cnd]]

The Chin delegation arrived at Panglong on the night of 6 February 1947. Its members were U Hlur Mung (Falam), U Thawng Za Khup (Tiddim) and U Kio Mang (Haka). [[iseas-panglong]] The Agreement of 12 February 1947 was signed by the Chin, Kachin and Shan with the Burmese leadership under Aung San. [[bni-cnd]] Burma became independent on 4 January 1948. [[bni-cnd]]

Thang Za Tuan’s day-by-day account adds the first Panglong conference of March 1946, attended by Pum Za Mang, Hlur Hmung and Kio Mang. On 26 and 28 March the British official Stevenson urged the hill delegates to stay with Britain; on 29 March the hill peoples agreed a union, with Hlur Hmung giving the first Rs. 50. [[thangzatuan-pzm]] For 1947 he names Thawng Za Khup, sent by Pum Za Mang, with Hlur Hmung and Kio Mang, says they arrived on 2 February, met Aung San on 9 February and signed on 12 February. [[thangzatuan-pzm]] [[thangliampau2010]]

Dates differ: the published Panglong documents place the Chin delegation’s arrival on the night of 6 February; Thang Za Tuan gives 2 February.

The 1947 constitution did not give the Chin full statehood, unlike the Kachin and Shan. [[frontier-cnd]]

Chin National Day, 20 February 1948 {{doc}}

A general assembly of Chin representatives voted on 20 February 1948 to replace hereditary chieftainship with a democratic system. [[chankhar]] [[frontier-cnd]] Accounts of attendance differ, from more than 500 delegates [[frontier-cnd]] to about 5,000 representatives. [[chankhar]] The Chin Affairs Council designated the date Chin National Day on 9 October 1950. [[chankhar]] The military government banned the celebration in 1994, requiring “Chin State Day” instead. [[frontier-cnd]]

Armed movements {{doc}}

The Chin Independence Army was formed in 1961. It was followed by the Chin National Front, most often dated to 1988, though some accounts trace its origins to 1985. [[shan-panglong]] [[wiki-zonat]] In India, the Mizo National Front launched an armed insurgency in 1966. [[lalengzuali2024]] [[newslaundry2015]] After the 1988 democracy uprising, Chin students who fled to Mizoram helped fill the ranks of the Chin National Front. [[iiss-northwest]]

The insurgency years left deep scars. Scholars of Mizo social memory note that sexual violence against women and abuse of minors during the insurgency and counter-insurgency remain part of the community’s collective memory. [[chakraborty2010]]

Reunification movements {{doc}}

T. Gougin (1925/26–2012), author of History of Zomi [[gougin1984]], built several organisations. He is credited with founding a United Zomi Organisation in 1961 [[thangsuanhau]]; another source gives a United Manipur Zou Organisation in 1959 [[khalvontawi]]. He founded the Zomi National Congress at Daijang on 28 January 1972 to campaign for the unity of the Zomi of India and Burma. [[khalvontawi]] [[wiki-zoro]] The Zo Reunification Organization (ZORO) was founded at the First World Zomi Convention in Champhai, Mizoram, on 20 May 1988, as a forum for reunification through peaceful, democratic means. [[wiki-zoro]] A declaration made at Chawngchhim, Champhai, on 19 May 1988 committed the movement to non-violence. [[virthli]] Since 1999 ZORO has engaged with UN indigenous-peoples bodies. [[wiki-zoro]]

The Zomi Re-unification Organisation (ZRO) was formed in April 1993 under Tedim and Paite leadership; its armed wing, the Zomi Revolutionary Army, followed in 1997. [[satp-zra]] [[wiki-zra]] In August 2024 ZORO mediated talks between rival Chin armed groups to de-escalate fighting. [[bni-zoro2024]]

How many people fled after the 2021 coup? {{doc}}

By mid-June 2021, Mizoram Police counted 9,247 people from Myanmar sheltering in the state, including the Chin State chief minister. [[pti2021]] Families who later moved abroad are covered on the Diaspora page. Violence in Manipur since 2023 has displaced many more people. [[asianews]] [[deccan2023]]

By mid-June 2021, Mizoram Police data counted 9,247 people from Myanmar sheltering in the state, including the Chin State chief minister, Salai Lian Luai. [[pti2021]] A Chin community organisation, citing UN OCHA, reported 46,200 people displaced within Chin State, and estimated about 47,200 refugees from Chin State in India. [[chinmd]] In July 2025 one report counted over 33,000 arrivals since 2021 [[statesman]]; in August 2025 state officials told the Assembly that over 29,000 were then sheltering. [[asianews]] Civil society and churches in Mizoram opened their doors, citing shared ancestry. [[asianews]] Fighting among resistance groups in Chin State has itself caused new displacement. [[statesman]]

Manipur, since May 2023 {{doc}} {{contested}}

A living conflict. This section reports figures and widely documented events only. Causes and responsibility are disputed by the communities involved. We do not take sides. Reading across sources from all communities is strongly encouraged.

On 3 May 2023, violence broke out between the Meitei majority of the Imphal Valley and the Kuki-Zo communities of the surrounding hills. [[reuters2023]] [[aljazeera2023b]] It followed a “Tribal Solidarity March” protesting a Manipur High Court order on the Meitei community’s demand for Scheduled Tribe status, which Kuki-Zo and Naga groups opposed. [[aljazeera2023]] [[aljazeera2023b]] By June 2023 the state government said over 50,000 people from both communities had been displaced. [[deccan2023]] On 22 November 2024 the state’s security adviser put the death toll at 258, including militants. [[pti-tribune2024]] [[siasat2024]]

Both communities report grave harms and contest each other’s narratives, including claims about migration from Myanmar; one fact-check found the claim that all Kukis arrived as refugees in 1968 to be untrue.
