Zomi History Glossary: Puan, Khuado, Zogam

Words you’ll meet on this site, explained simply. Each one links to where you can read more.

Bungpui
A banyan tree. The great banyan at Khampat is remembered as a symbol of the ancestors’ old home in the valley. In 2016 India asked Myanmar to help protect it. [[wire2016]] Stories
Chhinlung / Khul
The cave the ancestors are said to have come out of, at the start of Zo history. Kuki tradition calls it Khul; Mizo and Chin traditions call it Chhinlung. [[haokip2026]] Origins
Chin
A name for many Zo peoples, used for Chin State in Myanmar. It came into use through Burmese, not Zo languages, but many people now hold it with pride. [[carey1896]] [[zec-who]] Names
Chin Hills Regulation
The British law of 1896 that ruled the Chin Hills through the local chiefs. [[chinhillsreg1896]] Colonial Era
Chin National Day
20 February. On that day in 1948, Chin representatives voted to replace rule by chiefs with democracy. [[chankhar]] Modern History
Ciimnuai
A village near Tedim remembered as the first home of the northern Zo in the hills. The British recorded it as “Chinnwe” in 1896. [[carey1896v2]] Stories
Kabaw Valley
A river valley in Myanmar where historians think Zo ancestors lived before moving up into the hills. [[lehman-inscr]] Origins
Khampat
A town in the Kabaw Valley remembered as a Zo home before the move into the hills. [[chakraborty2010]] Origins
Khuado
The biggest Zomi festival of the year: a harvest and New Year celebration, usually in October. [[wiki-khuado]] [[mitv2019]] Culture
Kuki
A name for related peoples in Manipur and nearby. It was first used by Bengalis, not by the people themselves, and is now a strongly held identity. [[lalhmingliana2018]] [[guite2019]] Names
Lai
The self-name of people around Hakha and Falam, recorded by the British in 1896. [[carey1896]] Names
Laipian
The religious movement started by Pau Cin Hau, which teaches worship of one creator. [[pandey2010]] [[aeon2024]] Faith
Mautam
When the bamboo flowers all at once, rats multiply and eat the crops. The mautam of 1959 caused famine in the Lushai Hills. [[newslaundry2015]] Stories
Mizo
“Zo people.” The name used in Mizoram, India. [[wiki-zoro]] [[act1954]] Names
Panglong Agreement
The 1947 agreement in which Chin, Kachin and Shan leaders agreed to form the Union of Burma together. [[iseas-panglong]] [[bni-cnd]] Modern History
Pasian / Pathian
The creator in traditional Zo belief, and the word for God today. [[thawn2026]] Faith
Pau Cin Hau
A healer and teacher (1859–1948) who created a Zo writing system. His alphabet was added to Unicode in 2014. [[pandey2010]] [[unicode7]] Faith
Puan
Hand-woven cloth, made on a backstrap loom. Each area has its own colours: Tedim’s are black, green, white and red. [[mcdougall2018]] Culture
Siampu
A village priest who led sacrifices and festivals in the old religion. [[thangtungnung]] Faith
Zo
The shared ancestral name of the Zomi, Chin, Kuki, Lai and Mizo peoples. In 1896 the British wrote it as “Yo.” [[maninindia]] [[carey1896]] Names
Zogam
“Land of the Zo”: the hills of Chin State, Manipur, Mizoram and the Chittagong Hill Tracts. [[wiki-zonat]]
Zomi
“Zo people.” A name chosen in 1950 and adopted by the Zomi Baptist Convention in 1953. [[thangsuanhau]] Names
ZORO
The Zo Reunification Organization, founded in 1988 to bring Zo people across borders together peacefully. [[wiki-zoro]] [[bni-zoro2024]] Modern History
Zou
A tribe of southern Manipur and the Chin border. Not the same as “Zo.” [[kipgen2020]] Names
Zou Gal
The Zou name for the 1917–1919 war against British rule, also called the Anglo-Kuki War. [[thangboizou2020]] [[scroll-zaman2023]] Colonial Era
