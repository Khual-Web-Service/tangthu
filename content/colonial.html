The Colonial Era

Before the 1890s, Zo chiefdoms governed themselves. Within a decade, British columns had annexed the hills and divided them among Burma, Assam and Bengal — lines that became today’s international borders.

{{short}}
  • Around 1890, the British took over the Zo hills by force and split them between Burma, Assam and Bengal. Those lines became today’s borders. [[wiki-chinrising]] [[pau2019]]
  • From 1896 they ruled through the local chiefs under a law called the Chin Hills Regulation. [[chinhillsreg1896]] [[wiki-tlangpi]]
  • In 1917–1919, when the British tried to send men to work in the First World War, chiefs fought back. Zou people call it the Zou Gal. [[thangboizou2020]] [[scroll-zaman2023]]
{{/short}}

Annexation, 1889–1896 {{doc}}

The Chin-Lushai Expedition of 1889–90 brought the hills under British control for the first time. Chin chiefs resisted taxation and disarmament at various points from 1890 to 1896 before being disarmed. [[wiki-chinrising]] [[carey1896v2]] The British organised a Northern Chin Hills (Sukte, Kam Hau and Sizang areas) under Bertram Carey at Fort White and a Southern Chin Hills under David Ross at Haka, later making Falam the civil headquarters. [[wiki-chinrising]] [[carey1896]]

The officers who led the annexation also wrote its history. Carey and Tuck’s two-volume The Chin Hills (1896) and Reid’s Chin-Lushai Land (1893) remain essential primary sources — and should be read as the conquerors’ perspective. [[carey1896]] [[reid1893]]

The 1892 conference and divided hills {{doc}}

At the Chin-Lushai Conference at Fort William in 1892, officials debated amalgamating the newly acquired hills, which had been split among three administrations; the plan failed, and the Manipur–Chin Hills boundary was demarcated as a result. [[pau2019]] Mizo writers note that the question of a single administration “was never reopened again”; later unification demands trace instead to the Mizo Union’s 1946 resolution. [[virthli]]

The Chin Hills Regulation, 1896 {{doc}}

The Regulation provided the legal framework for governing the Chin Hills through indirect rule. [[pau2019]] Rather than abolish chieftainship, the British ruled through existing chiefs. [[wiki-tlangpi]] British Political Officers administered the hills from 1892, and Superintendents under the Regulation from 1896 until 1948. [[chinhillsreg1896]] [[wiki-supt]]

The Labour Corps and the Zou Gal, 1917–1919 {{doc}}

During the First World War, Britain sought labourers from the hills for France. Resistance became the uprising British sources called the Kuki Rebellion and many historians now call the Anglo-Kuki War (a name some Naga groups dispute); in Zou it is remembered as Zou Gal. [[scroll-zaman2023]] [[thangboizou2020]] The war lasted three years and ranged across more than 6,000 square miles of Manipur, the Naga Hills, the Chin Hills and the Kabaw Valley. [[guite2019]] [[wiki-kukiwar]]

Chiefs feared both the overseas service and the loss of men needed to cultivate their fields, and saw recruitment as a threat to their authority. [[thangboizou2020]] [[wiki-kukiwar]] Zou chiefs made joint resistance with the Thadou (Haokip) of Ukha and Mombi. [[thangboizou2020]] A related Haka uprising took place in the Chin Hills in 1917–18. [[guite2019]]

The Zou village of Hiangtam near Singngat served as a headquarters of Zou fighters; its chief Goulun led the resistance there. [[thangboizou2020]] [[wiki-hiangtam]] Fighters used muzzle-loaders, flintlocks and leather hand-cannons (pumpi), examples of which survive in the Pitt Rivers Museum, Oxford. [[prm-pumpi]]

Detailed military histories based on archival sources include S. Thangboi Zou’s chapter on the Zou Gal. [[kipgen2020]] [[thangboi]]

Contested memory {{sch}}

How the war is named and commemorated is itself debated. One commentator accuses unnamed groups of trying to “sideline or belittle the Kuki-Zo histories.” [[outlook-pitt]] Another criticised the 2017 Zou Gal centenary for being paired with a memorial to Maharaja Chandrakirti of Manipur, on whose orders the Zou chief Go Khaw Thang was killed. [[sabrang]] One view holds that Bengali nationalists encouraged the rising [[sabrang]]; others centre the forced recruitment for the Labour Corps. [[scroll-zaman2023]] [[thangboizou2020]] Readers are encouraged to consult the edited volumes, which include multiple community perspectives.
